# I. Title and Purpose

The objective of the Project Spotlight document is to act as a template and provide guidelines for generating a “Project Spotlight” post on LinkedIn for the HAAG organization. This document exhibits a pipeline with four phases. These four phases are comprised of sourcing, content collection, translation, and promotion. This pipeline even includes blank placeholders for specific steps of the pipeline so that anyone could use this template and follow it with limited oversight.

# II. Scope and Use

# The use and scope of this word document cover the protocols and procedures of the Project Spotlight feature. This feature will get posted onto LinkedIn. The word document also goes over methodologies for drafting and creating a post for the HAAG project spotlight feature. This word document will not cover the procedures of Faces of HAAG. It also does not talk about the post-feedback approval process.

## *Worked Example — Real Project Spotlight (Filled Draft)*

**Predicting Railroad Crossing Delays with Machine Learning**

**Visual:** A photo will be placed on top of the post

**Hook:**

The fact that people wait at railway crossings has an impact on public transit, commuters, and emergency services; in busy shipping cities like Charleston in South Carolina and Savannah in Georgia the ability to predict these delays can contribute to a safer and more efficient transportation system.

Isaac A. Felix, Leonardo Savasta and Xu Che, all of whom are researchers at the Georgia Institute of Technology, are studying how machine learning can be used to predict blockages at railroad crossings even when the transportation data is limited or noisy.

**Body:**

The Challenge: Getting reliable data on traffic bottlenecks is difficult since the information is generally obtained from sensors which never have complete accuracy or from incomplete reports regarding bus operations. The team hope to find out whether machine learning can still produce useful predictions in such real-world circumstances.

**The Methods:**

To address the challenge of predicting railroad crossing delays with noisy and incomplete data, the project will use several analytical and machine learning methods:

- The Bayesian Hierarchical Model will use the bus inference data to estimate the probability that a railroad crossing will be blocked and to predict how long the blockage is expected to last. It considers the uncertainty and variability that are inherent in the indirect observations of buses, which allows it to produce reliable predictions even when the data is incomplete.
- The Gradient Boosting Model will use the TrainFo sensor data from railroad crossings to predict both the probability and the duration of blockages, and it is able to capture the complex, nonlinear relationships between the sensor measurements and the crossing delays.
- Spatial and Statistical Analysis: The study makes use of GIS visualization, carries out correlation analysis regarding sources and crossings, and applies statistical significance testing to gain a better understanding of how blockage interactions occur throughout Charleston's transportation network. Further insights were obtained into freight-caused traffic delays and the factors that influence railroad crossing blockages through exploratory analysis of the distributions of blockage durations and temporal trends.

The use of spatial analysis together with predictive modeling will not only allow for accurate predictions of delays but also lead to a more detailed understanding of how congestion at railroad crossings spreads throughout Charleston's transportation network.

The Results:

The analysis uncovered several important insights:

- The sensor data from TrainFo is available at only a few railroad crossings.
- At some of the crossings the sensor readings are so noisy that a reliable analysis cannot be carried out.
- The information obtained from bus inference data does have value, but it is affected by the bus routes and by the surrounding traffic.

Looking Ahead: In future research the focus will be on comparing camera data with sensor observations in order to detect noise, using GPS and bus data to separate the signal from the noise, looking into further hierarchical modelling techniques, and improving the predictions as to both the duration and the probability of blockages.

**Credit:**

Isaac A. Felix, Leonardo Savasta, and Xu Che, Georgia Institute of Technology.

**Close:**

To sum up, the secret to more intelligent transportation is more intelligent data; this project promotes the development of transportation networks that are safer, more resilient, and more efficient by integrating transportation science, spatial analytics, and machine learning.
