# I. Title and Purpose

The objective of the Faces of HAAG document is to act as a template and provide guidelines for generating a “Faces of Haag” post on LinkedIn for the HAAG organization. This document exhibits a pipeline with four phases. These four phases are comprised of sourcing, content, collection, translation, and promotion. This pipeline even includes blank placeholders for specific steps of the pipeline so that anyone could use this template and follow it with limited oversight.

# II. Scope and Use

 The scope of document covers the procedure and protocols of the Faces of HAAG feature that will get posted on LinkedIn. It covers the methodologies for creating and drafting a post for a Faces of Haag feature. This document does not cover the post-feedback approval process, nor does it go over the procedure of the project spotlight.

# III. Roles and Responsibilities

- Sourer- identify prospective candidates and keeps track of who wants to be featured or not

- Draft Writer- Drafts and make revisions of the post

- Featured Person- provides feedback and comments about the draft

- Publisher – posts the draft onto LinkedIn, tags the featured person as well

- Faculty, Team leader, Bree – Signs off their approval of the post

- HAAG organization members – engages with and reshares the post on LinkedIn

# II. Standard Pipeline

Our pipeline has four phases for the project spotlight: Sourcing, Content Collection, Translation, and Promotion.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| PHASE | **What Occurs** |
| **1 · Sourcing** | Selection of a person within the HAAG organization |
| **2 · Content Collection** | Perform outreach and do a short Q&A session |
| **3 · Translation** | Translate the answers from the Q&A session into a LinkedIn post |
| **4 · Promotion** | Publish the post, tag it, and do a follow-up |

# III. Phase 1 — Sourcing

Choose a researcher, advisor, or faculty member. The candidate can also be a new team member speaking on why they joined. Candidates can also be pulled from HAAG leadership/project-lead recommendations, the HAAG project registration sheet, and the HAAG Summer Enrollments spreadsheet. Finally, also preserve an ongoing list of future projects and candidates to have a sustainable content pipeline, along with keeping track of who and which project has already been spotlighted or opted in or out.

# VII. The Post-Feedback and Approval Process

At this stage, the writer will wait for feedback about the project spotlight draft from members of HAAG, researchers, or faculty. Please use the post feedback and approval process for the Faces of HAAG in the “Faces of HAAG Post Feedback Process” word document. Do this process for every single draft before anything gets posted on LinkedIn.

# VIII. Phase 4 — Promotion

Publishing is the midpoint, not the finish line.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Step** | **What To Do** |
| **Tagging** | Tag the person. |
| **Resharing** | They share it themselves, in their own voice. |
| **Engagement** | Invite HAAG Slack to engage; someone from HAAG responds to comments. |
| **Reuse** | Eligible as a baseline for a future blog post. |

# XII. Outputs or Success Criteria

- Success of the procedure would be receiving feedback of the draft on time and getting it published onto LinkedIn with active engagement from users of the website.

# XII. Lessons and future improvements

- Need to know to who has accessibility to publish the draft onto LinkedIn.
- Need to assign roles and responsibilities ahead of the semester. To keep the process consistent and move along the semester.
- It was difficult in getting feedback for drafts. There should be a way to address this issue.
